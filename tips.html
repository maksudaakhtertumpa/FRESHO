Fresh Tips

Practical, everyday advice for choosing fresher produce, storing groceries longer, eating healthier, and keeping your shopping budget under control.

STORAGE

Keep leafy greens crisp for longer

Wash, spin dry, and wrap leafy greens loosely in a paper towel before storing in a perforated bag. The paper towel absorbs excess moisture that would otherwise cause wilting.

SHOPPING

Shop the edges of the store first

Fresh produce, dairy, and meat are usually along the outer aisles. Filling your cart here first naturally crowds out less healthy, heavily processed items from the centre aisles.

BUDGET

Plan meals around what's on sale

Check the week's offers before writing your list. Building 2-3 meals around discounted produce or proteins can meaningfully cut your weekly grocery bill.

HEALTHY EATING

Pick a colour from every group

Different coloured fruits and vegetables carry different nutrients. Aiming for at least three colours in your basket is a quick way to diversify your diet without counting anything.

STORAGE

Don't refrigerate tomatoes

Cold temperatures break down the membranes inside tomatoes, turning the texture mealy and dulling the flavour. Keep them at room temperature, out of direct sunlight.

SHOPPING

Never shop on an empty stomach

Studies consistently show hungry shoppers buy more impulse items and more calories overall. A small snack before you shop can noticeably shrink your final bill.

BUDGET

Buy pantry staples in bulk

Rice, oats, canned goods, and other long-life staples are usually cheaper per unit in larger packs. Only do this for items you know you'll use before they expire.

HEALTHY EATING

Read the ingredients, not just the label

Front-of-pack claims like "natural" or "light" aren't strictly regulated. The ingredients list and nutrition panel on the back tell you what's actually in the product.

STORAGE

Store potatoes and onions apart

Onions release gases that speed up potato sprouting when stored together. Keep them in separate, cool, dark, well-ventilated spots for the longest shelf life.

BUDGET

Use your freezer as a safety net

Freeze bread, meat, and even some fruit before it turns. Turning "about to go off" into "frozen for later" is one of the simplest ways to stop paying for food you throw away.
